Case File No.: GANO. F. 17Y. Op. 3. D. 809
Subject: Concerning the Discovery of the Body of Wealthy Merchant S. K. Zolotaryov
Case Status: Closed

Report
At eleven o'clock in the evening on November 24 of this year, a report was received from I.{NON_BREAK_SPACE}I.{NON_BREAK_SPACE}Koznyshev, a citizen employed as a train attendant by the Snezhnaya Royal Railway Transport Bureau, stating that passengers and crew aboard the train had witnessed the appearance of a vengeful railway spirit known as the "Ghost Bride," which allegedly dragged the prominent merchant S.{NON_BREAK_SPACE}K.{NON_BREAK_SPACE}Zolotaryov out of a carriage and brutally murdered him.

Examination Record
The cause of death was determined to be a fall from the train. No other injuries indicative of violence inflicted by a third party were found, nor were any traces of drugs detected. Examination of the scene revealed no evidence of pushing, dragging, or a physical struggle. As no Vila were aboard the train in question, the possibility of the crime having been committed by a perpetrator while airborne was also ruled out.
The reporting party and fellow passengers unanimously maintained that the incident was an act of "the Ghost Bride's revenge." According to local folklore, Karenina Vasilyevna, a theater actress who died seventeen years ago, took her own life by lying upon the railway tracks in her wedding gown after being spurned by her lover. It is said that she subsequently became a vengeful spirit, claiming the life of any unfaithful lover who travels by train.
In light of the findings of the on-site examination, the death shall be ruled accidental.

Findings Upon Review
The case has been determined to be one of intentional murder, concealed under the guise of a vengeful spirit. The supporting evidence is as follows.

Evidence of Tampering with the Compartment Window and Safety Lock
Findings: Examination of the sliding window on the track-facing side of the compartment revealed that its safety latch had been deliberately filed down. A thin metal shim had been inserted to jam the locking bolt, allowing the window to be pulled open easily from inside even while the train was traveling at high speed. A high-hardness cutting tool, with fragments of material still clinging to its blade, was recovered from the handbag of the deceased's wife, Grusha. The wear marks on the tool matched the scratches on the compartment window lock exactly.
Remarks: The window's safety mechanism had been deliberately disabled beforehand by someone inside the carriage, making it possible to push the deceased from the train.

Fragments of a Wedding Gown and Pulley Mechanism Found on the Train's Wheel Bogie
Findings: Investigators recovered a torn piece of lightweight silk from the train's undercarriage and exterior hooks, together with specially made fine iron wire and a one-way pulley. Examination confirmed that the apparent bloodstains on the fabric were not human blood, but artificial blood commonly used in stage productions.
Remarks: The apparent fall of the "Ghost Bride" from the train alongside the deceased was achieved through a stage magic technique. Using a pulley-and-counterweight mechanism installed outside the window, the perpetrators had arranged for a dummy dressed in a false wedding gown to be bound to the deceased and pulled from the speeding train along with him.

Inheritance Declaration and Voluntary Gift Agreement Signed by Grusha (Documentary Evidence)
Findings: Originals and copies of the Northland Bank Joint Property Trust Agreement and the Voluntary Gift Agreement signed by Grusha were recovered from the respective residences of the deceased and his mistress. The agreements stipulated that, upon Zolotaryov's death, Grusha, as his lawful first-priority heir, would inherit all shares in the businesses held in his name and transfer forty percent of those shares to Valeriya without compensation.
Remarks: The motive for the murder has been established.
