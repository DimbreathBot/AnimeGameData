<title name = Grand Exhibition of Prime Ice Sculptures to Be Held in Snezhnograd/>
<image preview name = UI_Img_SnezhnayaNewspaperPage_Pic_03/>
<center>Grand Exhibition of Prime Ice Sculptures to Be Held in Snezhnograd</center>
A grand exhibition of Prime Ice sculpture art will soon open in Snezhnograd. Organized with the Snezhnograd Veche's continued dedication to enriching the cultural lives of the people and guided by its careful deliberation, the event will feature the special exhibition "Classics, Redux: The Glaciafruit Master Returns (A Special Homage Exhibition)." Jointly arranged by the Veche and the Prime Ice Art Alliance, the exhibition offers citizens and all admirers of Snezhnayan culture an opportunity to witness the artistry of a master and gain a deeper understanding of the nation's cultural traditions.

More than a rare artistic occasion — one that comes only once in a decade — the exhibition also marks an important milestone in bringing Prime Ice art closer to the people. According to Ms. Kustodiev, Acting Chairwoman of the Prime Ice Art Alliance, the history of this art form has long been defined by its immense cost: "For many years, the creation of Prime Ice sculptures remained an art reserved exclusively for the court. During the era of the Pale Star Court, a poem recorded the reality of those times: 'To the halls of nobles were delivered countless Prime Ice sculptures; yet the miners of Zolnyy could not claim even a bowl of red beet soup.' Those words remain a true reflection of history."

Ms. Kustodiev further remarked: "The arts of Snezhnaya have undergone a profound transformation since Her Majesty the Tsaritsa ascended the throne. What was once the privilege of the aristocracy alone is now shared among the people. Prime Ice sculpture, however, remains a special case. Its unique nature makes it difficult for such works to enter every household. The Veche's decision to hold this exhibition reflects its commitment to Her Majesty's benevolent ideals, as well as its care for the people of Snezhnaya and its equal regard for all."
The Snezhnograd Veche likewise emphasized that an exhibition rich in historical and cultural significance can unite the people and inspire their devotion to Snezhnaya's glory. Thus, even amid these days of fierce winds and heavy snowfall, the decision to hold such an exhibition is considered both worthwhile and necessary.

While the Veche has yet to announce the exhibition's exact date, preparations are already underway. This newspaper has learned that commissions for the exhibition's artworks have been sent to Svetloledovka, where local Prime Ice designers have begun their work. Among them is Malevich, a designer from the town, who told our reporter that he was immensely proud to have been chosen. For him, the significance of this opportunity lies not in any special distinction, but in the fact that he is one of the countless ordinary people of Snezhnaya.

<title name = Tales of the Strange and Incredible: A Duke's Confession (Contributor Story)/>
<image preview name = UI_Img_SnezhnayaNewspaperPage_Pic_09/>
<center>Tales of the Strange and Incredible: A Duke's Confession (Contributor Story)</center>
To all who read these words, I am Duke Trokhin. My beloved kin, my graceful and cherished wife, my countless servants... all were claimed by the flames. I alone remain, condemned to endure this wretched existence and to record the sins of the one who brought this tragedy upon us.

"Let all who harbor dissent be seized from wherever they conceal themselves. Let each be examined and judged, and let those deemed guilty be removed by whatever means are deemed necessary. Thus is this order given."

Sorin the arsonist carried out the oppressor's command, bringing my family to ruin through the cruelest of means. Their cries rose as the flames consumed them. The fire itself answered with a dreadful melody, while their twisted, blackened forms became a final dance of agony. That vision returned me to the memory of our last gathering...

How I long for that night... While our oppressor slept beneath the cover of darkness, we gathered beneath the same sky and lit the lamps of celebration. We danced... we sang... paying no heed to the bodies oppression had left hardened and unmoving...

And then... the exile came. We were punished for the joy we dared to show, for the dances we shared, for the obedience we abandoned, for the knees we refused to bend. Within that splendid prison, we found solace in one another. We shared our hatred and stood united beneath the shadow of oppression. Yet when we reached the land of exile and the rains began to fall, we became the instruments of our own undoing. We turned against one another, and that became the fire that truly consumed us. Some even came to yearn for the oppressor — for the small sweetness he had once bestowed.

It was only when the flames touched my own body that I finally understood I was about to be consumed by the fire. But why? What terrible offense had I committed? Was it because I no longer wished to bow my poor spine and continue serving as a gentle support for those who sat so proudly above me?

Well... now I stand alone. With no one left beside me, another dance of rebellion is no longer possible. I am only a poor, lonely duke. What else can I do? All that remains is the rest of my life, and I shall spend it remembering the night when we danced together.
