<title name = Tales of the Strange and Incredible: Tales of the Strange From Okurov (Contributor Story)/>
<image preview name = UI_Img_SnezhnayaNewspaperPage_Pic_10/>
<center>Tales of the Strange and Incredible: Tales of the Strange From Okurov</center>
As everyone knows, a caterpillar spins a cocoon within a leaf and transforms into an ominous moth; the spawn of a frog lies in the muck, eventually sprouting legs to become a leaping, swimming frog. Ancient fae legends even held that when a person died, was buried, and rotted away, their spine would transform into a snake.

The way life begins is always different from how it ends. There is but one bird that proves the exception to that rule.

In the depths of a beautiful valley lives a lovely Firebird, so lovely that none who look upon it can tear their eyes away. Its wings, when spread, are so large they can blot out the sky; its eyes gleam like gemstones, while its feathers dazzle like gold. All who behold it cannot help but follow it deep into the valley, becoming to it beloved nourishment.

Of course, the Firebird does not feast on foul-smelling flesh, but rather upon memories.

The vast palace of Memory is the dwelling place of the soul. Without memory, a person ceases to be human. Once the Firebird devours their memories, they lose their senses, emotions, and speech, regressing into insensate trees that cluster around the valley town, shielding it from outside eyes. And the Firebird needs only wait — to wait for the next curious visitor to draw near.

Of course, time leaves the marks of age upon the Firebird, and sharp blades inflict their wounds. Death is never far from this beautiful creature, yet the promise of new life always favors it. When the breeze blows, its spent shell warms in the sunlight, and the flames of life ignite its magnificent plumage. It beats its wings amidst the searing inferno, while the falling ash nourishes the growth of fresh flesh and blood. Ultimately, the Firebird dons its resplendent attire once more, circling above Okurov, gazing quietly down at every passerby captivated by its beauty.

(Therefore, this writer sincerely recommends that all readers cherish their lives and stay away from Okurov. After all, unlike the Firebird, we only have one life to live!)

<title name = Watch Out for Insurance Scams, Keep Your Mora Safe/>
<image preview name = UI_Img_SnezhnayaNewspaperPage_Pic_04/>
<center>Watch Out for Insurance Scams, Keep Your Mora Safe</center>
"Solemn promises at purchase, but not a single Mora paid out during claims"? Rumors regarding Snezhnayan insurance traps have recently been circulating widely. Following an in-depth investigation, our reporters discovered that the cases in question all involve an entity named "Snezhnajoy Insurance."

Last year, a traveler from Mondstadt signed up for insurance with this company and bought "Prime Ice Construct Melt Insurance" for their Prime Ice fishing rod. The policy was as follows: If the insured item were to melt, the beneficiary would obtain up to 200,000 Mora. This traveler then returned to Mondstadt to fish, only for the rod to melt. The traveler went to seek recompense, only for the company to claim that this policy clearly stipulated that "the insured item must be proven to have melted within Snezhnayan borders," and as such, the traveler was not eligible for compensation.

This is not an isolated case. Recently, a Snezhnograd resident encountered claims issues related to this company's insurance products. This citizen, who wishes to remain anonymous, was originally quite an art enthusiast, but has found the Snezhnayan theater scene's output to have become of uneven quality. As such, she purchased this company's "Audience Trauma Insurance." However, when she did indeed view a truly terrible production, resulting in her being unable to drink her favorite Rimecurrant Puree, she attempted to claim compensation, only to be told by the company that to claim this insurance, the policyholder must prove that she genuinely suffered psychological trauma from watching the play, but the fact that she was able to come in person to file the claim indicated that she was mentally sound.

At present, Snezhnograd authorities have already filed an investigation into this company. At the same time, the Transport Bureau would like to remind all Snezhnayan citizens to be on alert against insurance traps and to keep their Mora safe. Additionally, for those who wish to purchase train delay insurance, we ask that you select the official insurance products offered by the Transport Bureau to safeguard your rights and interests.

New Teyvat Chronicles: Snezhnaya (V)
Historical Echoes — Fiction or Science?

In a previous installment, I posited that there are striking similarities between the pseudo-histories mainstream historians firmly believe in and the authentic historical records of Snezhnaya — similarities so profound that they can no longer be explained merely by cultural contact or the transmission of legends.

I want to begin by summarizing this phenomenon as "Historical Echoes": That is to say, documentary records that appear to come from different regions and eras, but which in fact are the same history, albeit repeatedly rewritten from different perspectives — with this hidden history being the chronicle of Emperor Monomakh's conquest.

During Emperor Monomakh's reign, most of Teyvat's regions were, in reality, under the rule of the same Tsardom.

Having read the Adventurers' Guild's reports, all nations today play host to ancient ruins that possess a relatively unified architectural style — that is, similar to that of ruins we have found in Snezhnaya. This is an incontrovertible fact. And this unified civilization, most evidently, was the realm of Emperor Monomakh.
