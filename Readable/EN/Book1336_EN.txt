<title name = Tales of the Strange and Incredible: The Clerk in the Interrogation Room (Contributor Story)/>
<image preview name = UI_Img_SnezhnayaNewspaperPage_Pic_09/>
<center>Tales of the Strange and Incredible: The Clerk in the Interrogation Room (Contributor Story)</center>
The clerk was curled up in the interrogation chair like a small beast, swaddled in a grey blanket that looked like the pelt of a rat. Though his body temperature had returned to normal, he still trembled incessantly, like a pine tree shaking snow from its needles.

When he saw my superior and me enter the room, the dark red uniforms of the Transport Bureau seemed to settle his frayed nerves just a little. Then, in a trembling voice, he began to speak, claiming that all of this was the will of His Majesty the Belyi Tsar...

It must be the will of His Majesty the Belyi Tsar, I swear it. So he began, before rambling on: It was the day before our departure. That morning, I didn't have my tie on. Well, not that I wasn't wearing it, but it had gotten wet and I'd left it on my desk. Just for that, I was... I was reprimanded by that gentleman. Oh, my superior, Mr. Kurlin: "Ubiritse, look at yourself! Do you think you're in your own parlor, or some tavern? How dare you leave your collar flared open like a butterfly? Do you have no respect for your post?"

I must have offended him somehow. I've always... done my work so poorly. In Mr. Kurlin's eyes, everything I do is always... riddled with blunders. Having been scolded again that morning, I felt I was about to be... cast out of the department. The mere thought of it made my brain feel as if it had shrunk inside my skull, rattling loosely. But how could any of this be Mr. Kurlin's fault? Before knocking off work, I meant to ask him what I had done wrong, and beg for his forgiveness.

I went to his office, but just as I was about to step inside, I heard him speaking: "Look at that Ubiritse! Every time he gets a scolding, he cringes like a frightened hare. How laughable. We have to head up the mountain for a field survey tomorrow, and that's when I'll really make him squirm! I'll... I'll tell him to go steal honey from a bear's den, chew him out for not preparing any meals, and then berate him for failing to scout the route ahead of time... Yes, that's what I'll do!"

Alas, what was I to do? All I could do was kneel before the Tsar's White Birch and pray to the spirit of His Majesty the Belyi Tsar. My body grew colder and colder, until I saw the shadow of the Belyi Tsar — a dazzling, radiant shadow. Weeping bitterly, I asked him how I could ever steal honey from a bear's den. The shadow told me not to fret, and gave me a cap that could cloak my form. Then I asked how I was to prepare meals on the snowy peaks. Oh, and His Majesty's shadow gave me a tablecloth that could conjure food from thin air. And at last, when I asked how to survey the route, the Belyi Tsar stroked my head and bestowed upon me a coil of rope, telling me that as long as I held it in my hand, the other end would point to the proper path.

Oh, merciful Belyi Tsar! Thanks to His Majesty's grace, everything went without a hitch the next day.

Mr. Kurlin said, "Go fetch the honey from the bear's den!" So I... I put on the cap that cloaked my form, stepped into the den, and brought back the honey.

Then he said, "Why didn't you prepare our meals beforehand?" So I laid out the tablecloth, and myriad delicacies emerged from within —skewered roasts, honey cakes, ice cream, rich stews, cold soup, you name it! Mr. Kurlin looked positively livid, yet he wolfed down every last scrap that appeared.

After that, he asked again, "What now? Where do we go from here?" So I took out the rope... and its other end reared up like a serpent, pointing toward the proper path.

Surely, I thought, Mr. Kurlin would finally have nothing left to hold against me. But his face was dreadfully dark. He glared at me for a long time, and went on, "Ubiritse, you did this on purpose to humiliate me! You... well, where is your tie? Looks like you're asking for a beating!" With that, Mr. Kurlin closed in. He raised his claws, claws that were... heh, bigger than my entire face.

Yet he couldn't hit me. He couldn't find me in the snowy mountains because I had put on the cap that cloaked my form. And then his body stiffened, for that feast beyond measure was also poison beyond measure. And then the rope... heh-heh, the rope was like a serpent. It darted from my hands and coiled... coiled right around his neck, heh-heh...

The Belyi Tsar... It must all be... heh-heh... the will of the merciful Belyi Tsar.

With those words, the clerk's eyes went wide, glinting with an eerie light. From that moment on, no matter how my superior and I pressed him, he never uttered another sound.

"So what really happened?" I asked my superior.

"None of that ever happened," he replied. "The Mr. Kurlin this gentleman speaks of had merely gone up the mountain alone today on a survey. The clerk slipped poison into Kurlin's food, strangled him with a rope, and lost his mind."

So that's how it is. We continued to discuss the case as my superior and I walked past the evidence room. Among the items impounded for the investigation, a coil of rope rustled inside its evidence bag. Perhaps it was only my imagination, but for a fleeting instant, I saw one end of the rope rear up like the head of a serpent.
