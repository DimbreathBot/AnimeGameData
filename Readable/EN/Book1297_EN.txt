<title name = Secret History Uncovered? Local Man Finds New Fae Race by Accident!/>

<center>Secret History Uncovered? Local Man Finds New Fae Race by Accident!</center>
A 30-page petition was recently submitted to the Snezhnograd Veche by Popolov (pseudonym), a destitute, long unemployed 40-year-old resident of Snezhnograd's Downtown District. In this petition, which was submitted out of the blue, the man claims that according to a tattered family tree he found in his grandfather's attic, he is no mere commoner as listed in municipal registry records, but rather, the descendant of a long-lost line of fae nobility.
Subsequent investigation revealed that Popolov, who previously served with the Fatui Skirmishers, was stationed at an outpost deep in the Sumeru rainforest. Then, during a routine march, he mysteriously vanished. Though he was eventually located and rescued by his squadmates, but he was then forcibly discharged by command shortly after for suspected mental instability. Upon returning to Snezhnograd, the former soldier secluded himself in his late grandfather's attic, subsisting entirely on his Fatui pension.
When a reporter from this paper stepped into the attic — a space heavy with the smell of fire-water and pickled cabbage — they found Mr. Popolov draped in a "nobleman's robe" fashioned from old curtains, seated regally atop a "throne" cobbled together from discarded clutter.
Mr. Popolov claims to belongs to a fae race known as the "Krepysh." While no surviving records of such a group have been found, his newly unearthed family tree indicates that his lineage is ancient, second in age only to His Majesty the Belyi Tsar. Although Mr. Popolov displays zero physical hallmarks of fae blood, his unusually massive physique does lends the claim a certain credibility. The document further suggests that the Belyi Tsar once elevated his ancestors to princely ranks, bestowing upon the clan the entire territory where Glupov stands today.
Mr. Popolov has reportedly filed a formal petition with the Veche, demanding the restoration of his lawful seat on the council under the ancient rights list in his family tree, and that years of accumulated back taxes e written off. Local residents informed our reporter that the aspiring noble also appears ready to peddle self-issued warrants to his neighbors and mint currency for his sovereign territory.
The Veche has yet to issue a response to the petition as of press time. Watch this space for exclusive updates as this story continues to unfold!

<title name = Tales of the Strange and Incredible: Horror in the Hills (Contributor Story)/>
<image preview name = UI_Img_SnezhnayaNewspaperPage_Pic_05/>
<center>Tales of the Strange and Incredible: Horror in the Hills (Contributor Story)</center>
Whether man or fae, people today instinctively cling to the belief that the world operates on a set of benevolent — or at the least, intelligible — rules. Yet, because our minds and intellects are but products of this world, from the perspective of logic and mathematics we can never truly apprehend the world's reality in full.

For us, this may well be the greatest mercy the world bestows. Once, I had a glimpse (may Her Majesty preserve my soul) of things so profane and abhorrent, no human should ever comprehend them. Words fail to capture what I witnessed, and no known language can articulate that primordial, maddening horror. I once swore that as long as I lived, I would never speak of what A. G. Pamiyev and I endured. I kept my silence, for people would have deemed me insane otherwise. Yet Mr. Pamiyev is now dead, and I shall soon follow him into the dark. Thus, I am compelled to publish the report that was withheld back then, if only to rest in peace in the thereafter. Should death erase my memories entirely, it will be a true blessing.

The report is as follows:

Horror in the Hills? Newlyweds Meet Mysterious End in Svetloledovka

The Royal Railway Transport Bureau of Snezhnaya recently uncovered the bodies of two young persons, who had been missing for days, just outside the estate of Viscount A. Pamiyev.

Reports confirm the pair were a newlywed couple (names withheld by request) who had ventured into the mountains for their honeymoon, only to meet a tragic end on territory held for generations by Snegovik nobility. Even veteran officers long accustomed to seeing the dead were left shaken to their core by the sheer horror of the scene.

In an interview, Viscount Pamiyev pointed to three major mysteries about the case that remain completely unresolved:

First: Footprints in the snow indicate a desperate flight for survival in the couple's final moments. Not only had their tent been shredded from the inside, they had also cast aside their heavy winter boots for some reason. With massive strides, they had sprinted wildly through the deep snow, as if fleeing some unimaginable terror. Yet across the vast, empty expanse, no other tracks existed besides their own frantic, bare footprints.

Second: In stark contrast to the various signs of a frenetic final flight, the deceased bore no trace of agony or terror on their faces. Instead, their features were frozen in a state of what can only be called profound peace, complete with smiles of unsettling, almost eerie satisfaction.

Third: Among the victims' belongings, police recovered a Kamera containing just one photograph, heavily blurred and flooded with an eerie halo of light.

Watch this space for further developments on this chilling case.

...

That was all to that account. The writer had omitted the obscure, nearly forgotten stories surrounding the Pamiyev bloodline. One chronicle, allegedly compiled during the 53rd fae era, claimed that the Pamiyevs were "cursed by a god," though the deity in question was certainly not the Tsar Monomakh. Most of these rumors were chilling, cryptic tales suggesting that a sinister, profane cult operated within the family. These tales were so horrifying, they made the infamous nobles of Fontaine and Mondstadt seem as pure as ascetics by comparison.

I first met Andrei Grigorovich Pamiyev five years ago. Back then, I too believed these stories were nothing more than crude superstitions...
