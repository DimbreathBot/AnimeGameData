Case File No.: GANO. F. 422. Op. 6. D. 1899
Subject: Investigation Into the Discovery of the Body of Cincinnatus C., a Staff Member of the Svetloledovka Administration
Case Status: Closed

Report
At dawn on March 15 of this year, a report was received from a train attendant of the Snezhnaya Royal Railway Transport Bureau. A body was found slumped in a seat on the right-hand side of a train traveling from Svetloledovka to the Snezhnaya central Station. The deceased was later identified as Cincinnatus C., a staff member of the Svetloledovka Administration.

Examination Record
The autopsy revealed purplish-blue discoloration and swelling of the face, as well as cyanosis of the oral mucosa. Distinct ligature marks and strangulation marks were visible on the neck, with subcutaneous and muscular hemorrhaging and a fracture of the greater horn of the hyoid bone. The findings were consistent with mechanical asphyxiation caused by external compression of the neck, resulting in airway obstruction and compression of the cervical blood vessels, leading to cerebral hypoxia and impaired venous return.
However, no murder weapon was found at the scene.

Based on the findings of the crime scene investigation, the death should be classified as a homicide. However, according to the train attendant's report to the Snezhnaya Royal Railway Transport Bureau, this was the first train service of the day. The train attendant clearly remembered that Cincinnatus C. had been the train's sole passenger.

Case Closure Report
This case is classified as intentional homicide. The perpetrators were Y. I. Luzhin and T. P. Punin, who acted in collusion.
The investigation established the following sequence of events:

On the evening of March 14, the day before the murder, T. P. Punin did not return to Snezhnograd, instead remaining in Svetloledovka.
In fact, the person who boarded the train with Y. I. Luzhin on the morning of March 15 was not T. P. Punin, but the victim, Cincinnatus C. By claiming that his friend had not yet arrived and asking the attendant to wait a little longer, Y. I. Luzhin led H. H. Kimbote, the train attendant, to believe that the two men were traveling together.
During the journey, Y. I. Luzhin strangled Cincinnatus C. to death with a scarf.
To prevent accidents, Train A, traveling from the Snezhnaya central station to Svetloledovka, and Train B, traveling from Svetloledovka to the Snezhnaya central station, proceeded slowly while passing each other in accordance with railway operating regulations. During this time, Y. I. Luzhin and T. P. Punin transferred Cincinnatus C.'s body from Train A to Train B through a window. T. P. Punin then climbed back onto Train A through another window.
In this way, they pulled off an identity-switching ruse: T. P. Punin was made to appear to have been traveling on Train B, while the body of Cincinnatus C., who had originally been aboard Train A, was transferred to Train B.

The evidence is as follows:

The Scarf That Y. I. Luzhin Failed to Destroy Completely
A local employee once saw Y. I. Luzhin burning refuse outside an abandoned wooden cabin in a remote part of Svetloledovka. The employee thought there might be something valuable inside worth salvaging, but found only some seemingly useless documents and half a woolen scarf.
Subsequent examination confirmed that the woolen scarf belonged to the victim, Cincinnatus C., and had also been the murder weapon.

The Unsigned Letter of Accusation
Shortly after Cincinnatus C.'s murder, an anonymous letter was delivered to the Snezhnaya Royal Railway Transport Bureau headquarters. The letter accused Y. I. Luzhin and T. P. Punin of having long profited from their positions by manipulating two sets of account books.
Handwriting analysis confirmed that the letter had been written by the victim, Cincinnatus C.

Case Closure Report
In the name of Her Majesty Anastasya Feodorovna Snezhnaya, and pursuant to the judgment handed down by the Court of Novokitezhgrad on June 3 of this year, Y. I. Luzhin and T. P. Punin have been convicted. The judgment has been carried out, and the case file is hereby archived.
