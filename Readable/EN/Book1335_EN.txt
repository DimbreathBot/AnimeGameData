<title name = Fraudsters Posing as Transport Bureau Detectives Seek "Authentication Details" — Distillery Staff Urged to Stay Alert!/>
<image preview name = UI_Img_SnezhnayaNewspaperPage_Pic_08/>
<center>Fraudsters Posing as Transport Bureau Detectives Seek "Authentication Details" — Distillery Staff Urged to Stay Alert!</center>
The Snezhnaya Royal Railway Transport Bureau recently seized a shipment of counterfeit Fire-Water traced to illegal distilleries. Following the trail of the illicit liquor through its distribution network, detectives successfully raided three unauthorized workshops involved in producing, transporting, and selling substandard alcohol, confiscating more than ten million Mora in the crackdown.

Counterfeit Fire-Water has long been a thorn in the side of Snezhnayan authorities, but this latest batch was an unusually convincing imitation. While the liquor itself left much to be desired, its packaging was another story. Everything from the shape and finer details of the bottles to their caps and the anti-counterfeiting prints on their labels was reproduced so faithfully that the fakes were practically indistinguishable from the genuine article.

So how did the counterfeiters manage to get every detail right? According to detectives, the criminals had done their homework before ever bottling a drop. Armed with forged credentials, they passed themselves off as Transport Bureau detectives conducting inspections into counterfeit liquor. Under the guise of gathering information to help identify fraudulent products, they approached unsuspecting merchants with questions about the finer points of product authentication. Eager to assist what they believed was an official investigation, the merchants not only divulged the details of their anti-counterfeiting designs but even handed over sample bottles. The criminals then used this information to manufacture convincing replicas of genuine Fire-Water bottles and packaging.

Authorities have once again reminded merchants that legitimate Transport Bureau detectives will never ask them to disclose confidential anti-counterfeiting designs, provide product samples, pay Mora in advance for an investigation, or purchase advice on how to circumvent Snezhnayan laws, regardless of the circumstances. All businesses are urged to remain vigilant and safeguard sensitive information against potential fraud.

<title name = Jumping from Windows into Snowdrifts Sounds Like Fun? Think Again Before Taking the Plunge!/>
<image preview name = UI_Img_SnezhnayaNewspaperPage_Pic_02/>
<center>Jumping from Windows into Snowdrifts Sounds Like Fun? Think Again Before Taking the Plunge!</center>
As temperatures continue to plunge, several days of heavy snowfall have brought snow accumulation to record levels. In an effort to keep roads open, street maintenance crews have been temporarily piling the cleared snow against the outer walls of residential buildings.

Some children, along with a number of Snezhnayan residents who appear to have retained their childhood recklessness, have spotted an opportunity for a rather dangerous new pastime. Their idea of fun involves leaping from upper-story windows straight into the snowdrifts below. While the thrill may be hard to resist, this seemingly harmless amusement has already sparked numerous disputes and left several people nursing injuries.

An investigation by this newspaper has uncovered three incidents involving this peculiar pastime in the space of just a few days.

In the first incident, a young girl identified as A, who lives in Zolnyy, jumped into a snowdrift without realizing that someone had placed a Prime Ice Launcher underneath. Rather than landing safely in the snow, she was promptly launched onto the roof of a building across the street. She remained stranded there for several hours before Transport Bureau personnel were alerted to her predicament and came to her rescue.

In another incident, a boy identified as B, also a resident of Zolnyy, leapt into a snowdrift only to discover that it was already occupied by a fae identified as C, who had drunkenly tumbled into the pile the previous day. The incident reportedly left an individual identified as C with a serious arm injury.

The third incident took place in Zapolyarny, where a man identified as D jumped into a snowdrift and accidentally disturbed a fae identified as E, who had fallen into the snow while intoxicated the night before. Fortunately, D landed beside the slumbering fae rather than directly on top of them, sparing E the kind of serious injury suffered by C in the previous incident. Unfortunately, the sudden awakening left E both startled and furious. Acting on instinct, the fae delivered a powerful blow to D, leaving the man with a severely injured arm.

This newspaper would like to remind readers that there is nothing wrong with seeking a little excitement or having some fun, but the pursuit of a moment's thrill should never come at the expense of one's own safety or cause unnecessary trouble for others.

<title name = Stolen Prime Ice Sculptures Recovered! Exhibition Organizer Hails Detectives' "Outstanding Efficiency"!/>
<image preview name = UI_Img_SnezhnayaNewspaperPage_Pic_09/>
<center>Stolen Prime Ice Sculptures Recovered! Exhibition Organizer Hails Detectives' "Outstanding Efficiency"!</center>
The Prime Ice sculpture exhibition, Classics, Redux: The Glaciafruit Master Returns (A Special Homage Exhibition), is currently in full swing in Svetloledovka.

However, the exhibition recently found itself at the center of a theft when several replicas of the master's works were stolen, with the culprit even attempting to smuggle them out of Snezhnaya. Fortunately, detectives from the Snezhnaya Royal Railway Transport Bureau got wind of the theft and sprang into action, apprehending the suspect just before they could make it across the border. The stolen sculptures were subsequently recovered and safely returned to the exhibition.

The exhibition organizer had the following to say in appreciation of the Bureau's efforts.

"Er... Yes, we're very grateful to the Transport Bureau detectives. Although... well, this is an exhibition of replicas, so the stolen pieces weren't actually worth all that much. And they're rather a hassle to deal with, so we'd already planned to give them away in a raffle once the exhibition wrapped up... Which is to say, we wouldn't really have minded if they'd gone missing..."

"But, um... anyway, our thanks to the Transport Bureau. You certainly can't fault them for how quickly they got things done."

Once again, the long arm of the law has proven that no criminal can escape its reach! With their swift and decisive response, detectives from the Snezhnaya Royal Railway Transport Bureau have shown that even if the stolen goods are nothing more than Prime Ice sculptures destined to melt shortly after leaving the country, they can count on the Bureau to recover them before so much as a puddle is left behind!
