<title name = Tales of the Strange and Incredible: Through Stars and Nightmares - Mr. Feodor's Time-Space-Defying Journey (Contributor Story)/>
<image preview name = UI_Img_SnezhnayaNewspaperPage_Pic_09/>
<center>Tales of the Strange and Incredible: Through Stars and Nightmares - Mr. Feodor's Time-Space-Defying Journey (Contributor Story)</center>
Word has reached us regarding a certain Mr. Feodor, a naturalist currently under retainer with the Adventurers' Guild. Though remarkably quiet and rather hopeless at small talk, he enjoys high esteem among his peers for his vast erudition and razor-sharp intellect. Even the Guild's most seasoned adventurers routinely find themselves humbled by his seemingly endless store of knowledge.
Yet, as only his dear wife knows, this seemingly omniscient expert was a jobless, heavy-drinking, middle-aged mess a mere five years ago. Quite what caused such a dramatic overnight transformation remains a total mystery to her, but she is utterly thrilled all the same, if only because it spares her from worrying herself sick day in and day out!
Then, on a summer day this past year, Mr. Feodor collapsed mid-lecture right in front of his eager adventurer pupils. Upon waking, he could no longer answer a single scholarly question and even claimed to have no recollection whatsoever of ever being a naturalist.
According to him, he had just spent five years trapped in an interminable nightmare. He claimed that a race of star-faring beings known as the "Chroniclers" had whisked his soul away to countless strange worlds, then eventually dropped him off in a Teyvat ruled by reptiles and lizards. It was only then that he realized he had landed in a primordial age unrecorded by history. Then, prompted by these strange folk, he duly jotted down a full five-year chronicle of his wild travels, then left the manuscript behind in a castle of lizards.
Shortly after, poor Mr. Feodor was packed off to a sanitarium for treatment. The good doctors there insisted he was merely suffering from a nervous affliction, and in time, the gentleman came around to their view, convincing himself that the entire affair was nothing more than a bad dream.
Yet just last month, a top-secret letter arrived from a band of adventurers who had once studied under Mr. Feodor. Deep within a ruin in Natlan, these daring fellows actually dug up the ancient manuscript he had once insisted he had authored... written, just as he described, from stone tablets of a peculiar kind.
And so our strange little tale draws to a close here. While Mr. Feodor's nightmare may never truly end, it leaves us with a far more chilling question: as you lay down in bed tonight, can you be entirely certain that the person sleeping beside you is still the very soul you remember?

<title name = Millennia-Old Ancient Wisdom for Our Times!/>
<image preview name = UI_Img_SnezhnayaNewspaperPage_Pic_07/>
<center>Millennia-Old Ancient Wisdom for Our Times!</center>
The latest research is in: Taking hot baths is bad for your health!
According to a recent study by the Society for the Advancement of Traditional Snezhnayan Medicine, the people of Snezhnaya did not take hot baths before Kresnik's Torch came into use. This is also why our forebears lived such long lives as recorded in our histories.
The authors of the study note that hot baths raise body temperature. As a result, the pores of the skin open, allowing harmful substances in the air that cause various ailments to enter the body. At the same time, hot baths also damage the skin's natural protective layer of microorganisms, thereby weakening immunity.
The Society for the Advancement of Traditional Snezhnayan Medicine hereby urges all readers to stop partaking in these harmful baths. Remember, good health is for both you and me!

<title name = A Statement From Ms. Anna Petrova/>
<image preview name = UI_Img_SnezhnayaNewspaperPage_Pic_08/>
<center>Mrs. Anna Petrova's Solemn Declaration</center>
I, Anna Petrova, hereby give notice that, as of this day, I have formally separated from Mr. Mikhail Petrov, and no longer acknowledge him as my lawful husband.
Mr. Petrov, having placed his full faith in the so-called Society for the Advancement of Traditional Snezhnayan Medicine's absurd study denouncing hot baths, has refused all forms of bathing for the past three months and has instead limited himself to merely wiping himself down with a towel. His present condition, and the foul stench resembling that of a swamp which now surrounds him, have become utterly intolerable.
For the sake of my personal health and safety, I have since left Mr. Petrov's place of residence, and I accept no responsibility for his actions henceforth.
