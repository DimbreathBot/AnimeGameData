The Envoy

(Act I)
(Scene: The reign of Emperor Monomakh. The mansion of Viscount Schpekin.
A snow-laden town, frozen in winter's grip. At its center stands a lavish estate, blazing with light.
Rear Backdrop: Towering, cold, impenetrable iron windows, sealed tight against the raging blizzard outside. Main Stage: The grand banquet hall of the estate.
Within the banquet hall, glasses clink and toasts flow in lively revelry. Swaying stage lights simulate gently swinging crystal chandeliers, casting a warm golden glow across the main stage.
Officials adorned in opulent attire, dripping with tassels and medals, glided across the floor alongside noble ladies in their finest splendor, moving together in a languid, hollow dance. An adagio.)

[Curtain Rises]
(Suddenly, the grand doors are hurled open by a gust of wild wind, sending a blizzard of snow sweeping inside. The loyal manservant, Buyanov, staggers onto the stage, holding high a letter sealed with wax.)

Manservant Buyanov: My Lord! Lord Spekin, an urgent dispatch from the Zapolyarny Palace!

(The music cuts short. In the background, the ensemble of dancers freezes mid-dance, locking into grotesque, rigid postures.)

[Variation: The Viscount's Panic]
(Viscount Schpekin unfolds the envelope with trembling hands and begins to read. As his eyes trace the lines, his dance, once proud and expansive, deforms into a morbid, contorted writhing, as though crushed by an invisible weight. Then follows a flurry of frantic spins, as if he were striving to flee the very parchment in his hands. At last, the Viscount halts on faltering feet, holding the dispatch high for all to see.)

Viscount Schpekin: My good sirs, I bring tidings of ill speed, foul, most disagreeable news!
Viscount Schpekin: An envoy, an envoy from Emperor Monomakh himself, is descending upon our town!
Baron Balannikov: What, an imperial envoy?!
Baron Yudintsev: What, an imperial envoy?!
Viscount Schpekin: An envoy from the Zapolyarny Palace, an envoy of the Pale Star!
Viscount Schpekin: He's travelling incognito, bearing the sacred decree of Emperor Monomakh!

[Group Dance: General Panic]
(The officials scatter like startled jackdaws in wild flight. Their group dance degenerates into utter chaos as they shove one another, cover their ears, or point fingers in fierce altercation.)

Baron Balannikov: Ruined! We are all undone!
Baron Yudintsev: But why, Your Excellency? Why would the Emperor's envoy come to inspect our town of all places?
Baron Balannikov: Fate! Cruel, unjust fate has sent him!
Baron Balannikov: Those envoys always went elsewhere in the past. Who could have guessed our turn would come today!
Baron Yudintsev: The foulest thing of all is this secret inspection under assumed names! Who knows which damned beggar he might pick out at random, egging them on to slander and condemn our every deed without restraint!
Baron Madeyev: But we are doing all this out of the goodness of our hearts!
Baron Yudintsev: Precisely, but those fools simply do not understand!
Baron Yudintsev: Take you, for instance, my beloved Anton Pavlovich. Your wife's little hound, in order to secure its first-place victory at the grand hunt, you have your servants feed it smoked sausage five times a day!
Baron Madeyev: Naturally! When it claims victory, which of our subjects would not weep tears of overwhelming emotion at such shared, collective glory?
Baron Yudintsev: You simply think too well of them, Anton Pavlovich!
Baron Yudintsev: What if some ungrateful drunkard goes to the envoy and claims he is starving all because of your incompetence?
Baron Madeyev: What?!

(Viscount Schpekin strikes his cane sharply against the floor, cutting short the tumult and uproar of the crowd.)
Viscount Schpekin: Enough! Fools! What is there to panic about?
Viscount Schpekin: As the old saying goes, do not blame the mirror if your face is crooked, and fear no shadow if your posture is straight.
Viscount Schpekin: If one acts with upright honor in daily life, why fear the gossip of the churls?
Manservant Buyanov: My Lord...
Baron Balannikov: My Lord...
Baron Yudintsev: My Lord...
Viscount Schpekin: Let him inspect all he pleases. What have we to fear?
Viscount Schpekin: Our compassion for the lowly rabble is as pure and clear as the snow outside the window, and our every word and deed is dedicated to the benevolence of His Majesty the Emperor.
Manservant Buyanov: Naturally!
Baron Balannikov: Naturally!
Baron Yudintsev: Naturally!
(The lighting dims slowly.)
Viscount Schpekin: All the same... we must find a way to ensure the envoy sees our good deeds as clearly as we do.
Manservant Buyanov: As clearly as we do?
Baron Balannikov: As clearly as we do?
Baron Yudintsev: As clearly as we do?

(Leaving their dance partners behind, the men dance over to the Viscount, huddling together in silent conspiracy. The curtain falls.)

?
(Act II)
Setting
(A magnificent countryside villa belonging to Viscount Schpekin, far removed from the bustle of the town.
The backdrop features a massive pane of luminous floor to ceiling glass. It is broad daylight, and outside the window lies a manicured, opulent garden landscape draped in snow. The main stage represents an extravagantly appointed study within the villa.
A roaring fire blazes in the fireplace, driving off the winter cold. Upon a massive, carved wooden desk sit overflowing piles of velvet purses, jewelry caskets, and thick stacks of calling cards.
Music begins, adagio. Dressed in white, the maidservant Poshlepkina sits as motionless as an ice sculpture in a velvet armchair, without uttering a word. Nearby, the blond youth Khlestakov lazily counts the loot upon the desk out of sheer boredom. Adagio.)

[Curtain Rises]
(Khlestakov stops what he is doing and turns around. He comically puffs out his cheeks, exaggerates an imaginary potbelly, and waddles over like a duck before bowing deeply to the maiden in white.)

Khlestakov: My humblest respects to the immortal Pale Star, and to you, special envoy!
Khlestakov: You have traveled all the way from that distant holy land of Snezhnograd to grace our tempestuous borderlands, and surely you must be worn out, body and soul. To subject a grand envoy to such a wretched inn in town is a total failure on our part!
Khlestakov: Allow me then to invite you to stay at my humble dwelling in the countryside.
Khlestakov: This is not merely to show my courtesy, but to offer you the ideal position to oversee the conditions of the common folk across this land.

[Variation: The Youth's Performance]
(Poshlepkina turns her face away slightly with a cold gaze, without uttering a word. The music shifts into a variation as the tempo quickens. The lights narrow and focus directly on Khlestakov.
His body shrinks instantly from bloated pride into groveling servility. He holds out a heavy jewelry casket in both hands, trembling as though it were a bomb ready to explode at any second, capturing all the affectations of Baron Yudintsev.)

Khlestakov: My revered lord, please do not spurn this humble, trifling gift!
Khlestakov: Look, if you will — it is a piece of the purest "ice sculpture," carved through the night by a few of our town's most deft and charming "children"...
Khlestakov: Though crude and unrefined, it bears an offering from every last one of our subjects, a devotion without a single trace of impurity!
Khlestakov: Our people beam with pride at the grand arrival of the imperial envoy, weeping rivers of joy just to catch a glimpse of the Pale Star's lingering radiance upon your person!

(The youth makes an exaggerated gesture of wiping away tears, then abruptly snaps upright, switching back to the cunning demeanor of the Viscount. Leaning on an imaginary cane, he slowly edges closer to the maiden.)

Khlestakov: And so, as the humblest shepherd to His Majesty Emperor Monomakh, and the most loyal servant to you, I, Viscount Schpekin, have a presumptuous request...
(The youth circles behind the maiden's chair and stoops over her like a viper, whispering into her ear.)
Khlestakov: I implore you, bearing the imperial decree, to tour our town and review his flock on behalf of His Majesty.
Khlestakov: To that end, I ask that you highlight our flaws and shortcomings, so that these loyal subjects, these stray lambs, may also have the honor of bathing in Your Excellency's radiant glory and grace!
Khlestakov: When that day comes, there will surely be long lines of people begging for an audience and your guidance, where countless tokens of "loyalty" as pure as gold, will gleam before you.
Khlestakov: Such a spectacle, I imagine, is not only what you and I wish to see, but also what His Majesty and the high lords of Snezhnograd long to behold.

[Duet: The Maiden's Inquiry]
(The youth steps back from the maiden's side and flings open the jewelry casket in his hands. A radiant blue light glares intensely even inside the sunlit study. Staring at the pile of gems, the youth lets out a crisp, mocking laugh. The music cuts off abruptly at its climax.
The maiden in white rises slowly, gliding beside the youth with elegant, delicate steps. She curtsies respectfully before Khlestakov.)

Poshlepkina: So, how do you intend to respond to the loyalty these people have displayed, my revered lord... "Excellency Khlestakov, Special Envoy of the Tsar"?
Khlestakov: Ahem... calm yourself, Viscount Schpekin. After all, who in this world is entirely without sin? Fate itself has decreed it so, a truth not even the mercurial Filipov could ever dispute.
Khlestakov: Yet sins vary in magnitude, and so do the paths to atonement. I shall present a faithful report to His Majesty, setting forth all the virtues and vices I have witnessed.
Poshlepkina: And what deeply moving "virtue," pray tell, did this loyal Viscount display to you?
Khlestakov: From what I have witnessed, his heart shines as bright as gold, his eyes are clear as Mora; and as for his retainers, they are like these velvet pouches stuffed with gems, leaving a most profound impression on me.
Khlestakov: Upon such barren and impoverished soil, this is exceedingly rare indeed.
Khlestakov: In return for this, I shall leave him a letter, and a promise.
Khlestakov: Pity shall be shown to him, just as he pities the poor; kindness shall be rendered to him, just as he treats the merchants; and grace shall be bestowed upon him, just as he pledges loyalty to His Majesty.
Poshlepkina: Exactly as he desires?
Khlestakov: Precisely! Exactly as he desires!

(The two strike a back to back pose in the center of the stage. The stage lights plunge instantly into blackness, leaving only the open jewelry casket emitting a faint blue glow in the dark. The curtain falls.)
?
(Act III)
Setting
(The stage is split in two: half bathed in light, half veiled in shadow.
The illuminated half reveals a grand banquet hall, peopled with noblewomen and officials clad in attire that appears modest but conceals immense luxury. Everyone holds a glass, yet the mood is far from festive; they whisper among themselves, seemingly awaiting the arrival of a crucial figure.
The darkened half holds the villa study from Act II, extravagantly opulent and hidden in the gloom.)

[Curtain Rises]
(Viscount Schpekin enters in high spirits, followed closely by his loyal manservant Buyanov proudly carrying a golden tray. Upon the tray rests a letter sealed in scarlet wax.)

Viscount Schpekin: My good sirs, I bring tidings of good cheer, glorious, most agreeable news!
Viscount Schpekin: The envoy, the envoy from Emperor Monomakh himself, along with his retainers, has departed from our town!
Baron Balannikov: Oh? The envoy has already departed?
Baron Yudintsev: Oh? The envoy has already departed?
Viscount Schpekin: Indeed, they have departed! They took our goodwill, boarded our finest carriages, and departed in utter delight!
(Viscount Schpekin draws the letter from the tray, holding it high for all to see.)
Viscount Schpekin: My dear friends, rejoice! I have brought back with honor the future promised by the envoy of the Pale Star! This, I believe, shall be the very future of our age! Rejoice, one and all!

(The music begins, allegro.)

[Group Dance: The Fools' Celebration]
(The atmosphere in the banquet hall shifts instantly into fervent delight. People press hands to their chests with deep sighs of relief, followed by a burst of wild, unrestrained laughter. The group dance unfolds in exaggerated revelry, with men and women taking hands to join the celebration. They raise their glasses in toast, slapping one another's empty pockets with comical glee, their faces beaming with the ecstasy of a triumphant investment as if the brightest future has already arrived.)

Baron Madeyev: At last, our goodwill bears fruit!
Baron Yudintsev: No longer need we live in fear, dreading that some damnable peasant might ruin our splendid life!
Baron Madeyev: His Majesty shall be well pleased with our benevolent rule, the Pale Star shall shine for us, and every subject, even the most wretched, brazen beggar, shall puff out his chest with pride at this glory!
Baron Balannikov: My dear Viscount, might we have the privilege of beholding the words left by His Excellency?
Baron Yudintsev: Yes, do let us see!
Baron Madeyev: Yes, do let us see!
Viscount Schpekin: Well, since you all insist...
Buyanov (Manservant): Then, if I may presume...
(Cuts him off)
Viscount Schpekin: No, no, no, Buyanov! The exalted words of His Excellency ought to be delivered from my own lips!

[Variation: The Phantom Thief's Mockery]
(Eerie yet lavish music swells as the lights bring the villa study out of the gloom. Poshlepkina, a maidservant clad in white, hunches over a desk writing a letter, while a blonde youth, styling himself Khlestakov, dances nearby. Allegro.)

Khlestakov: My esteemed Viscount Schpekin and honored colleagues, I am deeply touched by your warm hospitality. In you all, I behold hearts as radiant as gold, and eyes as clear as Mora.
Khlestakov: I have turned the matter over and over in my mind, pondering how to repay your benevolence, and at last the answer came to me: to show my mercy by relieving you of all those heavy "sins"!
(In the light, the officials' expressions begin to change subtly, their dance steps freeze.)
Khlestakov: I shall strip you of your fortune, just as you show pity to the poor;
Khlestakov: I shall lay bare your disgrace before the world, just as you show kindness to the merchants;
Khlestakov: And I shall leave you with a page steeped in mockery, just as you pledge loyalty to His Majesty!
(Poshlepkina rests her pen. The youth scoops up a handful of gems and scatters them into the air, turning toward the brightly lit banquet hall to execute an extravagantly sweeping doff of his hat.)
Khlestakov: And at the very last, I shall repay you with the selfsame sincerity you bestowed upon me, and reveal to you my true face. Greetings in the name of the ravens! Yours truly, the legendary thief Reed Miller!
(The shadowed lights snap off in an instant, and the illusion vanishes, leaving behind only the crisp echo of gems cascading to the floor. The music stops; the lit banquet hall falls into a deathly silence. Every gaze is fixed on the flimsy slip of paper in Viscount Schpekin's hands.
Viscount Schpekin slams his walking stick against the floor, trembling violently from head to toe, letting out a feral roar.)
Viscount Schpekin: Scoundrel...!!! Fetch the guards!

[Group Dance: General Panic]
(Music begins as the stage plunges instantly into utter chaos and terror. Officials and noblewomen scatter like headless chickens, every last one scrambling to slip away unnoticed. The group dance devolves into a comical yet desperate frenzy of escape, their aristocratic elegance vanishing without a trace.
At that very moment, from the depths of the stage comes the suffocating, synchronized march of military boots which halts dead in its tracks. A low, grim horn blares out, followed instantly by three soul-shattering thuds upon the door.
A column of Druzhna troops, blades drawn, marches into the hall as a blast of icy wind and snow surges in behind them, sealing off every path of escape. Presto.)

Druzhna Officer: By decree of His Majesty Emperor Monomakh, His Excellency Khlestakov, the imperial envoy descended from the Snezhnograd, has arrived in town! His Excellency commands all officials to present themselves at the inn forthwith!




(Viscount Schpekin awakens as if from a dream amid the laughter, staggering like a madman to the very edge of the stage. A harsh white spotlight strikes him as he frantically slaps his own face.)
Viscount Schpekin: Look! Look! Look upon this man, every one of you! See how an esteemed nobleman is made a complete fool of by a shameless thief!
Viscount Schpekin: See him bled dry of his hard-earned savings, reduced to an utter laughingstock!
Viscount Schpekin: See him dragged into a vulgar comedy by some shameless scribbler, turned into a pathetic clown for all to mock and despise! See them applaud and cheer at his disgrace!
(Viscount Schpekin thrusts his walking stick violently toward the audience, his voice turning hysterical.)
Viscount Schpekin: What are you laughing at? You are laughing at yourselves!!!
