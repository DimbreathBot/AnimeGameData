<title name = A Triumph for Security in the Capital! "Wandering Samurai" Who Terrorized Citizens for Weeks Brought to Justice!/>
<image preview name = UI_Img_SnezhnayaNewspaperPage_Pic_02/>
<center>A Triumph for Security in the Capital! "Wandering Samurai" Who Terrorized Citizens for Weeks Brought to Justice!</center>
Exclusive Report: The shadow over Snezhnograd has finally lifted. The Inazuman "Wandering Samurai," whose violent rampage kept the capital in fear for weeks, was captured in a pre-dawn operation yesterday.
For weeks, a masked Kairagi had been striking late at night across Snezhnograd's Downtown District and along secluded paths beyond Glupov, leaving the city increasingly fearful as the number of victims grew. Eyewitnesses described him as wearing battered crimson armor and a bizarre mask. Combined with his uncanny swordsmanship, his frightening appearance was reportedly enough to make several victims drop their belongings and flee before he could say a word.
Following a coordinated investigation led by the Criminal Investigation Division and Druzhna patrol units under the personal supervision of Snezhnograd's Police Chief, the culprit was successfully apprehended late last night. The investigation proceeded without interruption, with officers working day and night until the suspect was located inside an abandoned bakery in the Downtown District. When the fully armed culprit attempted to leave the premises to commit another crime, the patrol teams stationed nearby immediately took action and subdued him!
Investigations reveal that the criminal was a licensed merchant from Ritou in Inazuma. Due to an irreconcilable conflict of interest between the mercantile association and the Kanjou Commission in charge of the area at the time, said association was rendered bankrupt two years ago. Unwilling to revert to being a common citizen, the criminal, backed into a corner, stole crimson armor and a sacred blade that had been offered to a local shrine, then fled the country. First, the criminal fled to the border region of Nod-Krai and engaged in rank charlatanry by assuming the status of an Inazuman Samurai. After being exposed by the Curatorium of Secrets, he then sneaked into Her Majesty's sacred capital, Snezhnograd, planning to continue his deceptions. However, after discovering that he could plunder at will by relying on his terrifying attire, the offender repeatedly employed the same tactic. He was eventually apprehended by law enforcement after inciting city-wide panic.
As the criminal is a fugitive, the Inazuman authorities have refused to recognize his expatriate status. As such, he now awaits judgment under Snezhnayan law. For further updates on how this case shall progress, please keep an eye out for the latest news.

<title name = Tartuffe's Snezhnayan Travelogue/>
<image preview name = UI_Img_SnezhnayaNewspaperPage_Pic_09/>
<center>Tartuffe's Snezhnayan Travelogue</center>
(Editor's Note: This is a Snezhnayan travelogue written 138 years ago by the Fontainian author Tartuffe. It was later confirmed that this author never once left Fontaine.)
(Another Note: To prevent the reader from being affected by any misunderstandings due to the factually-incorrect content of the original text, the editor has marked out parts of the text that do not conform to Snezhnayan realities, or that might be easily misunderstood.)
...Our great continent hosts a realm favored by ice and snow. Once, it captivated the imaginations of many adventurers, yet the fear of the cold kept them at arm's length along the border. Consequently, the land became a vast, nebulous symbol, so much so that historians began to doubt its very existence. I, too, once gazed upon it from afar, or circled its periphery, until at last I conquered my fear of the unknown. And so I came to behold it. Through the train window, there it lay — Snezhnaya.
No one fears the extreme cold in Snezhnaya, for though the land is swept by endless blizzards, beneath it grow also combustible gems [1]. Humans are only one of the many forms of sentient life here. Even a hunting dog, a cat with its head held high, or even a large tree you happen to pass by might, out of the blue, speak in some ancient, elegant tongue[2].
The habit of drinking coffee is all but nonexistent in this land, for all things on earth can be brewed into fine wine. A certain noble invited me to try a local product known as "Kvass," which has some subtle similarity to Fonta. He told me that some princes of Snezhnograd once considered traveling to the Court of Fontaine, there to take up residence [3], but had dismissed the notion owing to the lack of Kvass in our lands.

<title name = Today's "On Ice": What Manner of Performance is This?/>
<image preview name = UI_Img_SnezhnayaNewspaperPage_Pic_01/>
<center>Today's "On Ice": What Manner of Performance is This?</center>
I recently went to see "The Seabird," staged in Snezhnograd — the worst program I've seen in decades! In terms of its presentation, it is neither a serious saga capable of evoking a sublime sense of grief in the audience nor a farcical comedy that provokes uproarious laughter.
And what, pray tell, shall the audience find in "The Seabird"? The show might hide behind such fanciful phrases as "avant-garde" or "realism," but they are mere smokescreens for the writer's sore dearth of stage proficiency! A hero is the main character, a figure no story should lack. But there are no heroes in "The Seabird." Our protagonists are a cowardly man, and a failure of a woman. They lack any active aspect, being wholly obedient unto fate, as though they were in spirit minor roles, unable to spark an actor's passion for performance.
Additionally, "The Seabird" forsakes all twists and turns worthy of a great work. Tepid progression interspersed with long pauses — that is the structural characteristic of this play. None sacrifice themselves for a noble cause, nor is anyone redeemed. Consequently, this work leaves one with little to discuss, and its plot and themes can be summed up in a mere two sentences.
And ballet! Ballet, the crowning jewel of Snezhnaya, how "The Seabird" has forsaken your hallowed traditions, and for what? The awful excuse known as "avant-garde." Not once does it give us a touching story, only displaying its loose structure, frustrating side characters, and every insignificant fly and insect. Alas! That this work should see the light of day at all brings me great agony indeed.
(Contributor: Trigorin)
