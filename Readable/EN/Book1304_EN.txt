<title name = Today's "On Ice": This is the Performance of an Era/>
<image preview name = UI_Img_SnezhnayaNewspaperPage_Pic_01/>
<center>Today's "On Ice": This is the Performance of an Era</center>
Previously, an article by Mr. Trigorin was published in this paper, criticizing the production of "The Seabird." This essay was quite the disappointment — for here we see how a critic's appreciation for deep art might be thwarted by his rigid, conventional views.
Mr. Trigorin's rejection of "The Seabird" on account of its mundanity and lack of heroes instead speaks to one reason why Snezhnayan ballet has failed to make any breakthroughs for many years. People demand only a few heart-stirring scenes, heaping praise only upon a handful of popular actors. Our so-called stagecraft amounts to nothing more than knowing the moments which shall elicit cheers from the audience and then stringing those scenes together into some convoluted tale.
Whether the onstage world is real and complete is not something that figures into the thought of figures in the art world, such as Mr. Trigorin. What "The Seabird" seeks is to shed the artificiality and shallowness of conventional ballet. The "long pauses" — dismissed with such disdain by Mr. Trigorin — that this work embraces constitute its most profound and powerful elements, for such pauses signify the tension, turbulence, and passivity of real life.
We cannot help but sympathize with these intellectuals, trapped as they are by ossified tradition. They deny all new approaches and new material by mere force of habit, even as they grind the courage to explore art into dust through shallow performance. "The Seabird" is a new thing, a thing that belongs to this era. The names of popular actors may be forgotten in times to come, but the truth of this time, which this work preserves, often proves immortal.
(Contributor: Tripolev)
