Welcome to Okurov!

We who live in Okurov have simple customs and get along with our neighbors. No matter where you're from, every door here is open to you, and all of us shall treat you as family.

Additionally, we have a most exceptional doctor in town, well-versed in the arts of herbal medicine and surgical excision alike, able to help you and your family banish any chronic ailments.

Welcome to Okurov! Let's build a happy home for tomorrow together!

The Seven Impossible Mysteries of Reed Miller, Thief Extraordinaire: The Platinum Piece
Chapter 11

...

"Me? My good sirs, what manner of farce is this?" Grand Duke Brahe Trubetskoy swept his gaze — at once amused and perplexed — over the police officers who were swarming around him like flies, fawning over him desperately. "All my servants can vouch for me! I haven't even stepped foot outside my estate these past few days, being occupied entirely with discussing refined Fontainian operas with Mademoiselle Milady de Este. Where on earth would I have encountered this Viscount Shuisky? Why, I haven't even glimpsed his shadow!"

The officers who had intended to curry favor with him were suddenly struck dumb, left only to exchange bewildered glances. Police Chief Duleyev — our most esteemed Chief — hastily removed his hat and recounted, with great care and in full detail, the events that had transpired: how the young Grand Duke had first arrived as Her Majesty's special envoy, how he had instantly seen through Viscount Shuisky’s ruses, how he had confronted the Viscount and knocked him unconscious with a single punch before the man could even draw his pistol, and how he had personally recovered the stolen platinum piece, and in an act of great mercy helped the locals bury the unfortunate Viscountess...

"Ah, that heartless, raving mad beast, that wicked bandit!" The police chief's face was flushed with intense emotion. "He even cut off one of his fingers, that it might allay suspicion of his guilt! He then hid the lost piece in his glove before speaking to us as if nothing had happened! He even went so far as to use some other poor soul's body to fool us, leading us to believe that some more terrifying serial murder was at play behind this case. Archon's sake, we dug through the snowy plains for months trying to crack this case! It was only thanks to your aid, fair Lord, that nothing further went awry..."

"Nothing went awry, you say!? You've all been deceived, you nitwits, you numbskulls!" The Grand Duke — the real one, this time — hurled his porcelain teacup to the ground, unable to contain his rage, roaring like thunder. "That wasn't me! That was that thrice-be-damned master thief, Reed Miller! He disguised himself as me and assumed my name so he could waltz into Shuisky's manor and take that platinum piece, precious beyond price, like one would fish for Mora in one's pocket! Shuisky had you dancing in the palm of his hand, and now Reed Miller's pulled the wool over your eyes... and you call yourselves detectives!?"

"But... But..." Chief Duleyev sweated furiously, unable to speak through the stutters, "Please, My Lord, please calm yourself... That man had your seal..."

"Fool! If some trifling gentry like Shuisky could fashion a platinum piece real enough to fool the eye, do you not think Reed Miller could forge my seal!?" Grand Duke Brahe Trubetskoy cried, incensed. Before, he had already been played all too often by Miller's beautiful female companion, one who named herself "Wagtail." Even the very mention of Miller's name was enough to give him a headache. He was now realizing that he was once again about to be the laughingstock of Snezhnaya. "What the hell are you waiting for? For the Fire-Water to drown your brains? Get out, and get my piece back, damn you all!"

<center>Snowland Sensations: Ode to the Snowy Plains (Contributor Story)</center>
(Editor's Note: This poem is a gift from Griboyedov of the Snezhnograd Writers' Guild to the Transport Bureau.)

You stretch out along the railway's path,
The white birches grow in your arms,
Oh, snow plains, you appear, so vast—
Endless, towards the horizon you surge!

The morning light shines brightly upon you, golden;
The moonlight by night keeps vigil over you, then falls into your embrace.
O snowy plains, our mother!
You envelop Snezhnaya's hills, you make our rivers flow.

This grace you bestow infinitely upon life,
Iron ore and sweet beets alike you give, fuel that grows upon you.
How shall we repay you? How your gentle generosity moves me!
Like a puny Kristall Bug, I prostrate before you in piety.
